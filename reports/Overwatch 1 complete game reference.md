# Public records chart Overwatch 1's server half

Public sources describe almost everything a final Overwatch 1 server did, but almost nothing about how it put that on the wire. Dated Blizzard patch notes, OW1-era wiki revisions, the OverPy Workshop catalogue and the OWLib/TACTLib data tooling together specify the rules, timers, hero numbers, maps, progression, cosmetics and custom-game settings of the final client in detail. Blizzard's GDC 2017 talks supply the architecture. The server simulates at 62.5 Hz and sends per-client state sync. Damage, health and team hostility are never predicted on the client. Kill cams are server-cut "reels" played in a second client world. No public source documents the 1.74.0.0.104319 transport or replication format beyond what this project has already verified on the original client. For the project, the record pays off in three ways. It gives a server checklist: every Workshop action is server-authoritative state the original server replicated. It gives calibration numbers: Tracer's reload is 1.0 s with ammo credited at 0.686 s, she drains 40 rounds per second, and OW1 training bots have 200 HP and 8-damage "Duo Cannons", 4 friendly and 11 hostile. It also explains the open problems. The hero HUD is on by default, so the gap is a missing context, not a missing switch. Teams, health and remote motion are ordinary replicated components. Gallery and hero-select previews are client-run presentation driven by lobby data. "Final OW1" means a January 2022 balance state shipped in an August/September 2022 client, and many current wiki pages describe Overwatch 2, so every number below is filtered for that. On the legal side, the US anti-circumvention exemption does not cover a reimplemented online server, and *Davidson v. Jung* upheld Blizzard's EULA against a Battle.net emulator. This report is not legal advice.

## Read every number against a January 2022 freeze

The sources fall into tiers. The most reliable are Blizzard's own dated texts: the patch-note archive, which still serves every OW1 live month from 2016-05 to 2022-09 ([Blizzard patch notes archive](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2022/09/)), forum announcements, and David Clyde's GDC 2017 slides ([Clyde slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2017/Presentations/Clyde_David_TheDataBuilding.pdf)). Next come wiki revisions pinned to the last edit before 3 October 2022, read through the Fandom MediaWiki API with `oldid` links, and machine-readable community data: OverPy at its last pre-OW2 commit `b24553ab` ([OverPy actions.js](https://github.com/Zezombye/overpy/blob/b24553ab0120ea4fcc040787e0b124ed617b9dda/src/data/actions.js)), and OWLib/TACTLib ([OWLib](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/tree/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28), [TACTLib](https://github.com/overtools/TACTLib/tree/129f977b604b2576bfd048e0f2be8b44ee983f39)). Last come Chinese reconstructions of the GDC talks, secondary press, and the 1.74 community server fork, which is reference-only because its original repository is gone and its license cannot be confirmed ([Boi-027 fork](https://github.com/Boi-027/overwatch-1.74-lobby-server)).

The report tags claims as follows. Untagged sentences with a citation are sourced facts. **[Inference]** marks reasoning not stated by a source. **[OW2]** marks a value documented only on Overwatch 2-era pages, or known to be OW2-only. **[Conflict]** marks sources that disagree. **[Project]** marks evidence from this repository's own runs on the original client, which is not public research but is the strongest evidence available for 1.74 internals.

The freeze date matters because hero balance stopped on **6 January 2022**, followed only by a Mercy Resurrect quality-of-life patch on 25 January ([January 6, 2022 Patch](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/January_6,_2022_Patch); [January 25, 2022 Patch](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/January_25,_2022_Patch)). Every later live patch through 6 September 2022 contained only events, cosmetics and bug fixes ([September 6, 2022 Patch](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/September_6,_2022_Patch)). Patches dated 26 April, 5 and 12 May, 28 June, 11 and 14 July and 11 August 2022 belong to the separate OW2 PvP beta. July 2022 wiki revisions had already absorbed beta kits for Doomfist, Orisa and Cassidy ([July 11, 2022 Patch](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/July_11,_2022_Patch)). The safe rule is to take hero data from April 2022 revisions where July ones were rewritten, and to treat any page edited after the OW2 announcement as suspect until a dated OW1 source confirms it.

## Match flow runs on a handful of timers the server owns

### Phases and timers

A standard attack/defense match moves through load-in, "Assemble Your Team" hero select, setup with attackers held behind spawn doors, the round clock, optional overtime, round end with side swap, match end, Play of the Game, and the cards/endorsement screens. Hero select starts only after loading; a February 2018 fix stopped players using it during the loading screen ([Patch notes Feb 2018](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2018/02/)). The final timer values are below.

Spawn rooms always have a healing zone and a barrier; enemies inside take damage until they die, and this is not credited as environmental damage ([Spawn Room, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Spawn_Room?oldid=121624)). Hero swapping is allowed in spawn, and Role Queue restricts picks to the queued role ([Competitive Play, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Competitive_Play?oldid=128194)). Non-competitive modes averaged respawn timers within 5 s of each other ("group respawn"); OW2 Season 12 replaced it with Wave Respawn and a 12 s base, which must not be used here **[OW2]** ([Respawn (current)](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Respawn)).

### Objective modes

Assault gives attackers **4:00 for point A and adds 3:00 on capture**, a June 2019 cut from 4:00 ([Patch notes Jun 2019](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2019/06/)). Capture scales up to three attackers. The bar has three ticks that act as checkpoints, and any defender on the point freezes it ([Assault, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Assault?oldid=123635)). Escort payload speed is per map. One pusher gives 100%, two give 116.5% and three give 133%. The payload rolls back after 10 s without attackers and heals nearby attackers **10 HP/s in OW1** (5 HP/s is an OW2 value) ([Escort, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Escort?oldid=125449)). Hybrid is an Assault point followed by two payload legs ([Hybrid, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Hybrid?oldid=125447)). Control is best-of-three. The point unlocks 30 s after both teams leave spawn and captures in 8/5/4 s for 1/2/3 players. The owner's score rises 1% every 1.2 s, and contact at about 99% forces overtime ([Control, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Control?oldid=121392)).

| Mode | Capture or push data (1/2/3 players) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Assault A | 30/20/15 s for three ticks (10/7/5 s per tick) | B: 18/12/10 s; decay rate back to last tick undocumented |
| Hybrid A | 32/21/15 s | payload unlocks after A |
| Control | 8/5/4 s; 1% per 1.2 s, so 120 s uncontested to win | no round clock |

Competitive used two mirrored rounds with time-bank extra rounds. A team had to reach **33.3% progress** for an Assault or Hybrid tiebreak to count. The March 2020 rule trimmed both banks equally until one reached 2:00. Teams were capped at one of each hero, and Role Queue fielded 2 tanks, 2 damage and 2 support from the 13 August 2019 beta (official in Season 18, 3 September 2019), with Open Queue re-added as a separate queue in Season 23 ([Competitive Play, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Competitive_Play?oldid=128194); [Patch notes Mar 2020](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2020/03/); [Patch notes Aug 2019](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2019/08/)). Sudden Death existed only in Season 1 and became draws in Season 2 ([Patch notes Sept 2016](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2016/09/)).

Arcade rules were simpler. FFA Deathmatch is 8 players to 20 points in 10 minutes, and Team Deathmatch is 4v4 to 30. A final blow scores +1 and a self or environmental death −1, and in TDM a Mercy resurrection removes the enemy's point ([Patch notes Aug 2017](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2017/08/)). The final Capture the Flag rules date from 8 February 2018: instant pickup, a 5 s lock on a dropped flag, a 4 s return not interrupted by damage, an 8-minute match to 3 captures, a 15 s flag respawn, a list of abilities that drop the flag, and Sudden Death with flags moved toward the center ([CTF updates, Feb 8 2018](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/overwatch/t/capture-the-flag-updates-february-8-2018/23)). Total Mayhem applies 200% health, 150% ultimate gain and 75% faster cooldowns, and Low Gravity applies 25% gravity ([Arcade, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Arcade?oldid=127559)). OW2's 5v5 format, Push, Flashpoint, Clash, role passives and the "50 wins to unlock Competitive" rule are all **[OW2]**; OW1 Competitive unlocked at player level 25 ([Patch notes Oct 2022](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2022/10/)).

### Ultimate charge

Every hero gains **5 points per second** passively and 1 point per point of damage to enemy heroes or healing done. Self-damage, damage to non-heroes, ultimate damage and damage to Adaptive Shield give nothing. Damage to Roadhog gives 75%, a change from 5 August 2021. Healing from The Reaping, Mercy's Regeneration and hacked packs gives nothing, nor does healing self or environmental damage. Mercy earns charge equal to the damage she amplifies. Charge has been spent instantly on activation since October 2017 ([Ultimate ability, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimate_ability?oldid=123286); [Patch notes Oct 2017](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2017/10/)). Whether charge carries between Control rounds or is lost on hero swap has no citable source **[Inference: commonly believed lost on swap]**.

### What the server must do, and which gaps this informs

A server needs a per-mode state machine (LoadIn → AssembleHeroes → Setup → InProgress → Overtime → RoundEnd → MatchEnd → POTG → cards/endorsements) with these timers, a respawn scheduler, objective controllers, and ultimate-charge accounting **[Inference]**. These numbers should be checked against the game-mode and ruleset records the client already holds (types 0xC5/0xC0/0xC6) and the statescript graphs those records reference, rather than hard-coded. The OW1 wiki itself concedes several gaps, such as decay rates and checkpoint additions. For the current Practice Range work, the relevant facts are that hero select follows loading and that hero swapping is a spawn-room privilege. The project's component-74 spawn-room flag and its 0x288B hero-select graph already reproduce both **[Project]** ([HANDOFF.md](https://github.com/p3n1cillin/ow1-preservation/blob/main/HANDOFF.md)).

### Generic formulas

Damage is computed as base × (1 + Σ dealt modifiers) × (1 + Σ taken modifiers) × headshot multiplier, with armor applied last ([Damage modification, oldid 120815](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Damage_modification?oldid=120815)). Armor subtracts 5 per instance but never more than half; beams lose a flat 30% instead; damage over time ignores armor. Healing restores health, then armor, then shields. Damage drains temporary pools first, then shields, armor and health. Shields regenerate **30/s after 3 s** without damage, in 5.76-point ticks every 0.192 s. Health shows "low" at 50%, "critical" at 25%, and a red screen edge and gasp at 35% ([Hit points, oldid 125959](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Hit_points?oldid=125959)). Headshots deal 2×; a fully charged scoped Widowmaker shot deals 2.5×. Deadeye and Tactical Visor always hit the body, and Fortify blocks critical damage ([Critical hit, oldid 124419](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Critical_hit?oldid=124419)). Small health packs heal 75 and respawn in 10 s; large packs heal 250 and respawn in 15 s ([Health pack, oldid 122064](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Health_pack?oldid=122064)). Base forward speed is **5.5 m/s (6.0 for Tracer and Genji)**, backward 90%, crouch 3 m/s. Within a category, buffs and enemy slows stack additively, but a self-penalty and an enemy slow do not stack ([Movement speed, oldid 117302](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Movement_speed?oldid=117302)). Every falloff pair in the tables fits a linear ramp from 100% at the near range to **30%** at the far range **[Inference]**. Damage, heal and shield ticks are multiples of 0.048 s and 0.192 s, which are 3 and 12 steps of a 16 ms simulation. That matches the 62.5 Hz server rate Blizzard described and the 62.5 command blocks per second this project measures **[Inference; Project]** ([lfzxb replay talk write-up](https://www.lfzxb.top/ow-gdc-replay/)).

The crowd-control taxonomy separates knockback, knock-up, stun, knock-down, sleep (broken by any damage), slow, pull, root, anti-heal, and hack/EMP, which also disable most passives ([Status effect, oldid 124015](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Status_effect?oldid=124015)). Environmental kills credit the last crowd-control source and count the victim's remaining health as ultimate-charge damage ([Environmental kill, oldid 115969](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Environmental_kill?oldid=115969)).

### What the server must do, and which gaps this informs

The server owns a damage pipeline with modifier stacks, armor and beam rules, a 0.192 s damage-over-time and heal-over-time tick, temporary health pools (the Workshop allows up to 16 per type per player), statuses, health packs and charge accounting **[Inference]** ([OverPy actions.js](https://github.com/Zezombye/overpy/blob/b24553ab0120ea4fcc040787e0b124ed617b9dda/src/data/actions.js)). These tables give the project ground truth for its responder's per-hero weapon parameters. The current baseline recipe uses a single `ReloadTime=1.4` and `FireHold=36` for Tracer, both of which disagree with the public numbers (1.0 s animation, ammo at 0.686 s, 40 rounds/s) **[Project]** ([HANDOFF.md](https://github.com/p3n1cillin/ow1-preservation/blob/main/HANDOFF.md)). The authoritative values are the client's own weapon and ability STU records. Public tables are the cross-check, not the source.

### Identifiers and coordinates

OWLib derives a map header (type 0x9F, raw prefix 0x0790) from the map GUID by keeping the low 32 bits: `(mapGUID & 0xFFFFFFFF) | 0x0790000000000000` ([OWLib MapHeader.cs](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/blob/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28/DataTool/DataModels/MapHeader.cs)). This matches the project's Practice Range header 0x0790000000000688 and map 0x0800000000000688. `STUMapHeader` carries `m_map`, `m_baseMap`, `m_supportedGameModes`, variant arrays, names and `m_mapType` (OffenseDefense 0, Mirrored 1, PVE 2) ([OWLib STUMapHeader.cs](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/blob/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28/TankLib/STU/Types/STUMapHeader.cs)). No public table maps gameplay header indices to map names. OWLib's name list covers only menu-background headers. OverPy's per-map "guid" values are not header indices: Practice Range's map and game mode both carry 0x2BA1, which marks them as display-name string GUIDs. Its variant ids, such as 0x0D80000000008F9D, are type-0x1C identifier GUIDs **[Inference]** ([OverPy maps.ts](https://github.com/Zezombye/overpy/blob/5a7d0e294b8cad73b9701987bb584d0551d7fa4d/src/data/maps.ts)). Objective volumes can be extracted from map placeables. OWLib picks each ENTITY placeable whose statescript instance data references the common objective graph **0x0580000000000E8E**, then outputs the matching AREA boxes ([OWLib ExtractMapObjectives.cs](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/blob/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28/DataTool/ToolLogic/Extract/ExtractMapObjectives.cs)). workshop.codes publishes community health-pack coordinates for every map; Practice Range has two small and two large packs ([workshop.codes health packs](https://workshop.codes/wiki/articles/health-packs-locations)).

### Practice Range and Tutorial

The OW1 Practice Range has no objectives. It holds **4 friendly and 11 hostile training bots** that stand still, shoot or move, and the ground carries range markers in metres ([Practice Range, 2019 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Practice_Range?oldid=95841)). An OW1 training bot has **200 HP**. Its "Duo Cannons" fire 8-damage projectiles at 60 m/s, four per second, from an 8-round magazine with a 1 s reload, and can headshot ([Training bot, oldid 123913](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Training_bot?oldid=123913)). The custom-game Practice Range mode exposes only Spawn Training Bots (on), Training Bot Respawn Time Scalar (10–500%) and Training Partner (on) ([OverPy customGameSettings.js](https://github.com/Zezombye/overpy/blob/b24553ab0120ea4fcc040787e0b124ed617b9dda/src/data/customGameSettings.js)). ### What the server must do, and which gaps this informs

Spawn rooms, doors, objective volumes, health packs and bots are placeable data in each map variant, and objective logic runs in graph 0x0E8E plus the mode graphs. A faithful server should therefore load these from client data, not invent coordinates **[Inference]**. This bears on three open items. First, "bots further into the range": the 15 bots should come from the Practice map's ENTITY placeables or mode graphs, not hand-picked positions. Second, Blink drift: collision-safe destinations need real geometry, which the server does not yet have. Third, the HUD: the Practice header's `m_supportedGameModes` list is the arbiter between the two candidate Practice modes, 0x023000000000005A (current recipe) and 0x0230000000000018 (fork claim).

### Levels, currencies and loot

### Competitive, career and social

Final OW1 Competitive (Season 35, with Season 36 announced for 1 September 2022) unlocked at level 25. It used SR 1–5000 across Bronze to Grandmaster, per-role SR plus one Open Queue SR, five placements per role, and Top 500 boards for each role, the combined average, and Open Queue ([Welcome to Season 35](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/overwatch/t/welcome-to-season-35-of-competitive-play/680081/1)). Matches paid 15 CP per win and 5 per draw ([Weird Gloop Competitive Points](https://overwatch.weirdgloop.org/w/Competitive_Points)). Season-end CP per role ran 25/50/100/200/300/450/650 from Bronze to Grandmaster, and 65 to 1,750 for Open Queue. Tier boundaries of 1,500, 2,000, 2,500, 3,000, 3,500 and 4,000 SR are widely cited but not confirmed by a primary source. SR decay was 25 per day for Diamond and above from August 2017, and whether it still applied in 2022 is unclear. The SR gain/loss formula was never published. The career profile exposed Quick Play and Competitive stats per hero in Combat, Assists, Best, Average, Game, Miscellaneous and Match Awards categories, plus level, prestige and endorsements; profiles defaulted to Friends Only from June 2018 ([overwatch-stats-api README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/overwatch-stats-api@2.1.0/README.md); [Patch notes Jun 2018](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2018/06/)). Endorsements (Shot Caller, Good Teammate, Sportsmanship) arrived in June 2018 with levels 1–5 that decay and drop to 0 on penalties; OW1 rewarded them with loot boxes. The per-match limit is recorded as 3 in 2018 sources but 2 on the current wiki **[Conflict]** ([Weird Gloop Endorsements](https://overwatch.weirdgloop.org/w/Endorsements)). Looking For Group let leaders set mode, voice, minimum endorsement and per-slot roles ([Weird Gloop LFG](https://overwatch.weirdgloop.org/w/Looking_for_Group)). Silenced players lost team, match, general and custom channels but kept group chat and friend whispers, which outlines the channel set ([Weird Gloop Penalties](https://overwatch.weirdgloop.org/w/Penalties)). Six annual events ran about three weeks each; 2022 had Lunar New Year, Anniversary (17 May–7 June) and Anniversary Remix Vol. 3 (9–30 August), with no OW1 Summer Games ([Weird Gloop Seasonal events](https://overwatch.weirdgloop.org/w/Seasonal_events)).

### What the server must do, and which gaps this informs

The server needs a durable account ledger. It holds XP, derived level and prestige, credits, Competitive Points and OWL tokens, loot-box counts by type, owned unlocks, per-hero loadouts, per-role seasonal SR, endorsements and persisted settings **[Inference]**. Loot generation can approximate the published odds, but duplicate protection and the post-2016 XP table are not public, so those are design choices, not preservation. The gallery's "0/0 cosmetics" most plausibly means the client is intersecting authored unlock lists with owned unlock records and finding nothing. The decisive test is to send the exact type-0xA5 GUIDs from each hero's `m_7846C401` lists. Run 203's "real default unlocks" may have differed in GUID set or record shape **[Inference]**. Event state, such as themed menus or event loot boxes, probably arrives as server configuration; the project's 20504/20505 key and hotfix lists are candidates. Without a configured event, the safe default is "no event" **[Inference]**.

The Overwatch-specific path is TLS on port 1119, then a WebSocket with protocol `v1.rpc.battle.net` and bindless RPC. The reply carries `ReferralInfo` with four key slots, `cid` and `hostv4` ([BGS_PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/p3n1cillin/ow1-preservation/blob/main/docs/BGS_PROTOCOL.md); [Boi-027 research](https://github.com/Boi-027/Overwatch-1-v1.74-Lobby-Research)). Plasmawatch implements the older v1 `ProcessClientRequest` form for an earlier build, and its repository embeds key material and a certificate that must not be copied ([plasmawatch/login-server](https://github.com/plasmawatch/login-server)). No public emulator implements Overwatch presence, friends or chat, and no source documents Overwatch rich-presence keys or whether party invites use BGS or the lobby.

### The lobby and matchmaking

Everything game-specific rides the Overwatch lobby protocol. Frames are `[u24 length][u16 msgid]`, where the message id is `(channel << 8) | (message − pool base)`. The bootstrap pool has CRC 0x11D82194, and about 105 protocol pools register some 922 messages in bands from 20300 to 60907 ([Boi-027 PROJECT_STATE.txt](https://github.com/Boi-027/Overwatch-1-v1.74-Lobby-Research/blob/main/docs/PROJECT_STATE.txt)). Prometheus's 0.8 analysis states that "main menu buttons only send JAM messages to the server saying 'hey i want to join a game'" ([saturn-xvi/prometheus](https://github.com/saturn-xvi/prometheus)). Keyless retail captures exist for custom-game, Quick Play queue and Arcade flows. Their message numbers belong to other builds, so only their grammar transfers.

Blizzard's own matchmaking statements set the design. Scott Mercer (2018) said the matchmaker targets equal-sized groups and does not create a game if either team's predicted win chance is below **40%** ([Groups and Matchmaking in Overwatch](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/overwatch/t/groups-and-matchmaking-in-overwatch/134776)). Group SR limits were 1,000 for Diamond and below, 500 for Master and 350 for Grandmaster (250 before September 2018) ([Competitive Play, OW1 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Competitive_Play?oldid=128194); [Patch notes Sept 2018](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2018/09/)). Server updates rose from about 21 per second to about 60 with the 2016 "high bandwidth" change ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/overwatch-netcode-tick-rate-60hz/)). Game servers ran on Blizzard's OpenStack cloud with autoscaling, and no public source describes the internal referral, allocation, backfill or data-center selection protocol ([OpenStack summit 2019](https://www.openstack.org/summit/denver-2019/summit-schedule/events/23379/how-blizzard-entertainment-uses-autoscaling-with-overwatch)). The Battle.net Agent listens locally on 127.0.0.1:1120, but the client can be pointed at any BGS endpoint with `--BNetServer`. Tournament mode (`--tank_TournamentMode --lobbyServer=`) bypasses BGS and zeroes the referral keys ([Boi-027 README](https://github.com/Boi-027/Overwatch-1-v1.74-Lobby-Research)).

### What the server must do, and which gaps this informs

A multi-player server needs one Balancer referral per client, one lobby session per client, one game-server instance per match with its own 20600 instance referral and keys, and a matchmaker that treats groups as atomic, enforces 2-2-2 in Role Queue and rejects lopsided teams **[Inference]**. Social services should be added only after the 104319 client's embedded descriptors confirm which listeners it registers. Report and block services can be stubbed to return success so UI flows complete. None of this explains the early instance failures, which no public source discusses.

## Tank's data model makes the client its own best specification

The engine is "Tank", reportedly built on reused Titan components ([39C3 Prometheus talk](https://media.ccc.de/v/39c3-prometheus-reverse-engineering-overwatch)). It runs several ECS worlds called entity admins (Lobby, LobbyMap, Game and Replay) ([owdev ECS](https://owdev.wiki/wiki/index.php/Game/ECS)). In the 0.8 beta, entity-id prefixes are 0x20000000 for player-synchronized entities, 0x40000000 for server entities, 0x60000000 for client-spawned projectiles from a lease block, and 0x80000000, the only prefix clients may spawn. The lobby admin's startup entity runs UI graphs 0x041A, 0x041B and 0x041C ([owdev Game/Structure](https://owdev.wiki/wiki/index.php/Game/Structure)). Data is StructuredData, a tagged binary format whose class and field names are case-insensitive CRC32 hashes. GUIDs pack 4 bits of engine, a 12-bit bit-reversed type field, platform, region and locale, and a 32-bit index. In 2017 the pipeline had 203 asset types, about 3M retail assets and 22k shaders. Retail loading is asynchronous from CASC/TACT, and packages can ship encrypted until content is released ([Clyde slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2017/Presentations/Clyde_David_TheDataBuilding.pdf)).

| GUID type | Content | Raw prefix (bits 48–59) |
|---|---|---|
| 0x002 / 0x003 | map / entity definition | 0x0800 / 0x0400 |
| 0x01B | statescript graph | 0x0580 |
| 0x01C | identifier (variants, events) | 0x0D80 |
| **0x025** | **game message** | **0x0240** |
| 0x026 | config var (unnamed in OWLib) | 0x0A40 |
| 0x058 | progression unlocks | 0x0EA0 |
| 0x05A | UXScreen (the project's "UX layers") | 0x09A0 |
| 0x075 | hero | 0x02E0 |
| 0x09F | map header | 0x0790 |
| 0x0A5 / 0x0A6 | unlock / skin theme | 0x0250 / 0x0A50 |
| 0x0C5 / 0x0C6 | game mode / ruleset schema | 0x0230 / 0x0A30 |

The table combines OWLib's type map with the bit-flip rule ([OWLib teResourceGUID.cs](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/blob/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28/TankLib/teResourceGUID.cs); [OWLib Combo.cs](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/blob/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28/DataTool/FindLogic/Combo.cs)). TACTLib reads Overwatch content under product code "pro". Build 104319 postdates 1.48, so it uses an encrypted CMF content manifest plus a TRG resource graph instead of APM packages, and TACTLib ships dedicated `ProCMF_104319` and `ProTRG_104319` key-derivation providers ([TACTLib ManifestCryptoHandler.cs](https://github.com/overtools/TACTLib/blob/129f977b604b2576bfd048e0f2be8b44ee983f39/TACTLib/Core/Product/Tank/ManifestCryptoHandler.cs)). Model files are chunked; the `MHRP` chunk holds hardpoints, and map chunks hold MODEL, ENTITY, AREA, SOUND and other placeables ([OWLib Chunks](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/tree/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28/TankLib/Chunks); [teMapChunk.cs](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/blob/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28/TankLib/teMapChunk.cs)). Rendering is deferred, with Enlighten lightmaps and probes; time of day is fixed at match start ([REAC 2025](https://enginearchitecture.org/downloads/REAC_2025_Overwatch.pdf)). Audio is Wwise with a threat-based "importance" mix and data-driven voice stimuli. Movies are Bink 2. AI uses per-mode behaviour graphs, difficulty profiles and "AI cell" and tactical-volume data, and no Blizzard talk covers OW1 bot AI ([OWLib STUAIBehaviorComponent.cs](https://github.com/overtools/OWLib/blob/546efa19516d7df7ba138802d221c73ad886df28/TankLib/STU/Types/STUAIBehaviorComponent.cs)).

Protection is undocumented by Blizzard. Community work on 1.74 describes Arxan-style protection: on-demand code decryption, executable pages that refuse VirtualProtect, and hardware breakpoints that crash the process. Read-only polling and heap writes are safe ([Boi-027 README](https://github.com/Boi-027/Overwatch-1-v1.74-Lobby-Research)). Blizzard's court filings name Warden as the anti-cheat ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/blizzard-is-suing-the-creator-of-an-overwatch-cheat-program)). The project measured zero dynamic-code (ACG) policy flags, so "ACG blocks page writes" remains unestablished **[Project]**.

The engine model says a reimplementation can let the client specify most behaviour. Gameplay lives in authored records and statescript graphs the client already holds, and the server mostly needs to replicate state and select the right records **[Inference]**. It also bears on two gaps. Weapon child entities are authored (`m_childEntityData` with type-0x3C hardpoints resolved against the model's MHRP chunk). The project's two world managers (startup and submitted) may be the live and replay admins **[Inference]**.

## Kill cam, POTG and replays are server-recorded reels

The only detailed text of Philip Orwig's GDC 2017 replay talk is a Chinese reconstruction, so its numbers are "as reported" ([lfzxb replay write-up](https://www.lfzxb.top/ow-gdc-replay/); [GDC Vault abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024053/Replay-Technology-in-Overwatch-Kill)). Overwatch uses state synchronization: per-client packets of per-object state, prioritized by relevance and bandwidth, delta-encoded against client-acknowledged history over custom UDP. Late joiners receive the "lifetime dirty set". Replay is two special receivers on the server, recording a full snapshot **once per second** and deltas at **20 Hz** (the server simulates at 62.5 Hz) into two buffers of about **1 MB per minute** each. A kill cam reel is the last snapshot before the reel start plus deltas to a point a few seconds after death. The server compresses it, fragments it to MTU size and sends it through a BlockTransferSystem within a roughly **0.5 s** budget, so it arrives before respawn. The client plays reels in a separate EntityAdmin while the live world keeps running, with a black fade between them. Game messages are annotated for replay and spectator inclusion; without that filtering, hit indicators, UI and voice lines went missing. Replays use default crosshairs because the replay domain lacks local settings. Dead heroes cannot win POTG, a rule added after a dead Torbjörn's turret won one. Turret kills, falls, environmental pushes and Dragonstrike get scripted cameras.

| Feature | Final OW1 behaviour |
|---|---|
| Play of the Game | categories High Score, Sharpshooter, Lifesaver, Shutdown (beta, 22 Mar 2016); weights unpublished; patented ([over.gg beta notes](https://www.over.gg/179/overwatch-beta-patch-for-march-22-2016)) |
| Highlights (June 2017) | "Today's Top 5" kept 24 h or until patch; manual 12 s capture, up to 36 stored; video export ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/you-can-now-save-your-overwatch-highlights)) |

A preservation server must record the streams it already sends (creates, statescript snapshots, movement) to support kill cams, POTG, highlights and replays. The client cannot reconstruct these on its own **[Inference]**. The talk also bears directly on the HUD. "First-Person Hero HUD" is very likely the label for the client's `Spectator_Show1PHeroHUD` setting, which means the hero HUD has a spectator-gated path. The project already found that graph 0x0BCF, which shows HUD_Root, is the highlight/replay viewer **[Inference; Project]**. Settings persistence belongs in the account store, returned through the 20802 preferences path the main menu already requires **[Project]**.

## Workshop is a public catalogue of server-authoritative primitives

The Workshop reached the PC PTR on **24 April 2019** and went live on **21 May 2019** ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2019/04/24/overwatch-custom-games-workshop-scripting-mode/); [Fandom Workshop citing May 2019 notes](https://overwatch.fandom.com/wiki/Workshop)). Later steps were Workshop maps and subroutines (February 2020), strings and C-style display (May 2020), a 32,768-element limit (October 2020) and Extensions (March 2021). The final OW1 catalogue in OverPy has **14 events, 222 actions and 265 values**. Only Create Projectile, Create Homing Projectile and Create Projectile Effect are **[OW2]** ([OverPy actions.js](https://github.com/Zezombye/overpy/blob/b24553ab0120ea4fcc040787e0b124ed617b9dda/src/data/actions.js)). Scripts are limited to 128 global variables, 128 player variables and 128 subroutines. Up to 16 health pools of each type can exist per player. Dummy bots are capped at 6 per team or 12 in FFA, and they "only move, fire, or use abilities if executing workshop actions". Only "Local Player" is client-side: "Update Every Frame" refers to server load and client interpolation, which confirms that everything else is evaluated on the server ([OverPy values.js](https://github.com/Zezombye/overpy/blob/b24553ab0120ea4fcc040787e0b124ed617b9dda/src/data/values.js)).

OW1 had three bot systems. Hero AI in Practice vs AI covered 12 heroes (Roadhog, Zarya, Bastion, Cassidy, Mei, Reaper, Soldier: 76, Sombra, Torbjörn, Ana, Lúcio, Zenyatta), with Easy/Medium/Hard dealing 25/50/100% damage, no headshots, 90% XP, and Assault, Control, Escort and Hybrid maps only ([Practice vs. AI, 2022-03 rev](https://overwatch.fandom.com/api.php?action=query&prop=revisions&titles=Practice_vs._AI&rvstart=2022-09-30T00:00:00Z&rvdir=older&rvlimit=1&rvprop=content|timestamp&rvslots=main&format=json)). Training bots were the third system. OW2's 5v5 AI, eight AI difficulties and wider hero AI coverage are **[OW2]**.

For the server, the Workshop list is the checklist of authoritative state the original server replicated. That means a status set plus a modifier stack (discord, nano, anti-heal and damage boost are multipliers, not statuses), input injection for bots, spatial queries over real map geometry, and per-viewer visibility for presentation entities **[Inference]**. A rule VM is needed only for Workshop parity. Bots are ordinary replicated players driven by server input, so a hero AI needs a navigation representation that the server may have to generate itself **[Inference]**. Two facts bear directly on open problems. Because "Disable Hero HUD" switches off a HUD "until reenabled", the hero HUD is **on by default** ([workshop.codes Disable Hero HUD](https://workshop.codes/wiki/articles/disable+hero+hud)). The legal sources are summarized here neutrally. This is not legal advice. The 2024 US §1201 exemption covers two uses. The first is restoring "personal, local gameplay" of lawfully acquired "complete games" whose servers were shut down. The second is on-premises preservation by eligible libraries, archives and museums ([eCFR 37 CFR 201.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-37?part=201&section=201.40)). The Copyright Office rejected remote research access in 2024 ([InfoDocket](https://www.infodocket.com/2024/10/25/u-s-copyright-office-rejects-dmca-exemption-to-support-game-preservation/); [VGHF](https://gamehistory.org/dmca-2024-statement/)). In *Davidson & Associates v. Jung* (8th Cir. 2005), Blizzard's EULA ban on reverse engineering was held enforceable and to waive the §1201(f) interoperability defence, and the bnetd Battle.net emulator was held to violate §1201 ([opinion](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/422/422.F3d.630.04-3654.html)). Blizzard's current EULA bans reverse engineering, emulating or redirecting its protocols "for any purpose", and connecting to emulated servers ([Blizzard EULA](https://www.blizzard.com/en-us/legal/fba4d00f-c7e4-4883-b8b9-1b4500a402ea/blizzard-end-user-license-agreement)). Enforcement elsewhere has ranged widely. Nostalrius closed after a cease-and-desist in 2016 ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/blizzard-speaks-out-about-nostalrius-shutdown)). Club Penguin Rewritten ended in police arrests in 2022 ([MassivelyOP](https://massivelyop.com/2022/04/14/disney-has-quashed-another-club-penguin-emulator-this-time-with-arrests-in-the-uk/)). City of Heroes Homecoming was licensed by NCSoft in 2024 ([MassivelyOP](https://massivelyop.com/2024/01/04/ncsoft-has-officially-granted-a-city-of-heroes-server-license-to-the-homecoming-crew/)). Blizzard won $8.56M against the Overwatch cheat maker Bossland ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/court-orders-notorious-cheat-maker-to-pay-blizzard-8-5-1794000244)). The EU "Stop Destroying Videogames" initiative collected 1,294,188 verified signatures, but on 16 June 2026 the Commission declined binding legislation in favour of a voluntary code ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/06/24/european-commission-stops-short-of-imposing-mandatory-requirements-following-citi-102n4yy)). OW1 almost certainly fails the "complete game" definition, and a reimplemented online server falls outside "local gameplay" as written **[Inference]**. AGPL-3.0 licenses only the project's own code, and §13 obliges any public network deployment to offer its source. The project's practices are the facts that would matter under *Davidson*'s reasoning. It generates its own keys, keeps original checks enabled, ships no client bytes, keys or dumps, and runs loopback-only. Commercial use and asset distribution are the aggravating factors in reported cases **[Inference]**. On source licensing, Prometheus is AGPL and upstream, and OverPy is GPL-3.0, which can be combined with AGPL-3.0, though using it for data only is simpler. Plasmawatch and the 1.74 fork are reference-only because they bundle keys or have an unconfirmable license.

## Conclusion

The public record changes the project's question. It no longer needs to find out what Overwatch 1 did: patch notes, pinned wiki revisions and OverPy specify that with unusual completeness, down to frame timings. The remaining question is which authored records and replicated components make the original client do it. That reframing has a practical consequence. Whenever a public number and the client disagree, the client's STU data wins, and the public tables serve as tests. The Workshop catalogue is the most useful single artifact here, because it is Blizzard's own enumeration of the server-authoritative state behind each player, written for designers rather than engineers.

The second takeaway is that the remaining gaps cluster around context rather than messages. The hero HUD is on by default. Hostility needs team bits. Remote bodies need continuous frames. Kill cams need recorded reels. The gallery needs owned-unlock records. None of these is a missing packet type. Each is missing state that the original server always supplied as a matter of course. That favours offline verification on dumps and authored data over further live black-box trials. Legally, the narrow footing argues for keeping the current posture: own keys, original checks, loopback only and no redistribution of client material.
